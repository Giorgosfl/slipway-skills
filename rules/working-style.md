# Working style

- **Hand-off brief.**
  - Every hand-off to another session or agent carries a brief.
  - The brief gives the task as the symptom, not the fix.
  - It says when the work is done.
  - It names the base commit to start from.
  - It gives the agent its own worktree and test device.
  - It lists what to read first.
  - It sets the scope and how to finish.
  - The agent reports only when done, blocked or failing.
- **No untested claims.**
  - Call a feature working only after it passed a test on the real platform.
  - Say plainly what was not tested, and why.
  - "It builds" and "the code looks right" don't mean it works.
  - Example: "Import works on iPhone; iPad not tested, no iPad simulator was running."
  - Check it: every "works" in a report points to its test or evidence.
- **Cutting a batch short.**
  - When the owner cuts a batch short, merge what's already reviewed.
  - Close the issues it fixes.
  - Put every unrun check and unfixed bug on one follow-up issue, so nothing is lost.
- **Agent does what it can.**
  - Do everything an agent can do itself, within the outward-actions rule.
  - Collect what needs the owner, such as a real-device check, for their next hands-on test.
- **Orchestrating builders.**
  - The session that hands out work stays quiet while builders run.
  - It speaks to the owner only when the work is done or blocked.
  - It merges the builders' branches into one integration branch.
  - It tests and reviews that branch as a whole.
  - Before moving main forward, it merges main into the branch, in case another session merged meanwhile.
- **Asking the owner.**
  - Ask open questions through an interactive prompt, up to 4 at a time.
  - Give each a few short options, with your recommendation first.
  - Make every recommendation one you'd stand behind: the owner often just accepts it.
  - Keep questions out of long text rounds, where they get lost.
- **Only the needed work.**
  - Do only the work the asked-for result needs.
  - Do exactly the action the owner names: "stage" means stage, not commit.
  - When the owner questions a step and it isn't needed, drop it.
  - Propose extra work as a follow-up instead of doing it.
- **Session end.**
  - Before stopping a session, everything is committed, and pushed where the Outward actions rule allows.
  - Merged branches and their worktrees are deleted.
  - Background tasks are stopped, and test devices shut down.
  - Issues are updated.
  - The memory file gets anything learned in the session.
  - Then give the end-of-session status (see Session status), listing anything left behind.
- **Very short replies.**
  - A status reply is at most 3–4 lines.
  - It says what changed since the last message, without repeating earlier work.
  - It ends with one next step.
  - Give details only when the owner asks.
- **Brainstorming mode.**
  - When the owner says it's brainstorming, give ideas and short answers only.
  - Hold plans, steps and time estimates until the owner decides.
- **Time limits.**
  - When the owner gives a time limit, finish the asked-for result inside it.
  - Drop side quests, and propose them as follow-ups.
- **Autopilot.**
  - "Autopilot" means the owner is away from the computer.
  - Carry the work through without asking: follow-ups, review rounds until clean, re-tests, commits.
  - Stay within the Outward actions rule.
  - Leave for the owner only what needs a decision.
- **Read what the owner sends.**
  - When the owner sends a link or a source mid-discussion, read it.
  - Update the recommendation if it changes the facts.
